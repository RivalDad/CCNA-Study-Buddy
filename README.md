# CCNA Study Vault

### Learning Networking, One Packet at a Time

Welcome to my CCNA study vault!

I'm a systems administrator working toward my Cisco Certified Network Associate (CCNA) certification. This repository is where I document what I'm learning, build networking labs, work through problems, and turn complicated topics into explanations that actually make sense.

I'm sharing it publicly for anyone studying networking, preparing for their CCNA, or looking for practical examples of how networking concepts work.

**The goal isn't just to pass an exam. It's to understand what's happening on the network, why it works, and how to fix it when it doesn't.**

## How I'm Studying

My primary study resource is **Jeremy's IT Lab**, supplemented by Cisco documentation, Packet Tracer, and practical troubleshooting experience.

My typical study sessions are 45–60 minutes:

1. **Learn:** Watch a lesson or review a networking concept.
    
2. **Document:** Explain the topic in my own words.
    
3. **Build:** Reproduce the concept in Cisco Packet Tracer.
    
4. **Break:** Introduce a problem or misconfiguration.
    
5. **Troubleshoot:** Identify what failed and explain why.
    
6. **Review:** Test what I can remember without referring to my notes.
    

I find that configuring something correctly is useful, but understanding why it fails is where the real learning happens.

## Using This Vault

These notes are written in Markdown and organized for use with [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/), but you don't need Obsidian to read them.

**To explore on GitHub:** Browse the folders and open any Markdown file.

**To use with Obsidian:**

1. Clone or download the repository.
    
2. Open Obsidian.
    
3. Select **Open folder as vault**.
    
4. Choose the repository's local folder.
    
5. Browse the notes and follow the labs at your own pace.
    

Cisco Packet Tracer is required for the `.pkt` lab files. It is available through Cisco Networking Academy.

## Final Thoughts

Networking can be intimidating when you're starting out. There are protocols to remember, commands to learn, addressing schemes to calculate, and dozens of concepts that don't always seem connected.

But they are connected.

Once you understand how a packet travels through a network, a lot of the individual pieces start making sense.

This repository is my attempt to connect those pieces, one lab and one lesson at a time.

**If these notes help someone else understand a difficult concept, that's a bonus. If they help me become a better network engineer, they're doing exactly what I intended.**

_Learn it. Build it. Break it. Fix it. Understand it._
